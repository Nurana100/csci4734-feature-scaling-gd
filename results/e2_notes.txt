Standardization slows down a lot as the features become more correlated. For linear regression it needs 6 steps with uncorrelated features, 114 at correlation 0.5, 894 at 0.9 and 8,081 at 0.99, and its condition number grows from 1.2 to 1,122. For logistic regression it goes from 81 to 548 to 2,540 steps and does not converge within the 20,000-step limit at 0.99.

Whitening is much less affected. For linear regression it needs only 3 to 14 steps in all cases, and its condition number stays between 1.0 and 1.9. For logistic regression it also slows down as correlation grows (73, 95, 151 and then 617 steps), but far less than standardization. The condition number of the logistic problem cannot be brought down to 1 by whitening alone (it rises from 4.9 to 75.5), because the logistic Hessian includes weights that depend on the model's predictions, and whitening only fixes the covariance of the features.

The reason standardization suffers is that it only fixes the size of each feature separately, but the correlation between features stretches the loss surface into a long narrow valley. Whitening removes that correlation, so the surface becomes much closer to round.

Min-max is the weakest scaler here. For linear regression it needs 6,155 steps with uncorrelated features and does not converge at correlation 0.99, and for logistic regression it did not converge in any setting. Without scaling, gradient descent did not converge in any setting.
