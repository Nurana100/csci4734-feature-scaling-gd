E3 learning-rate sweep (linear regression, scale ratio 100, rho = 0.5, lambda = 0.01, 29 learning rates from 1e-6 to 10, cap 5000 steps). Each curve stops near 2/L: standardize (2/L = 0.36) last converges at lr = 0.32, min-max (2/L = 0.53) also at 0.32, and whitening (2/L = 1.96) at 1.78, and the next learning rate on the grid blows up in every case. Whitening tolerates the largest learning rate (about 5 times more than standardize) and unscaled data the smallest, because its L is 12,001 so lr must stay below 0.00017, and with such small steps it never converged within 5000 steps; min-max converged at only one learning rate (3,287 steps) because its condition number is high (about 426). A larger learning rate helps because each step moves further, so the step count falls roughly as 1/lr (standardize: 3,800 steps at lr = 0.0056 down to 63 at lr = 0.32) and whitening needs only 2 steps at lr = 1/L, although right below 2/L it slows again (41 steps at 1.78) because it overshoots back and forth.
